Dad Reflex Privacy Policy

Effective 26 October 2026. This policy covers the Dad Reflex app for iPhone. The studio website has its own separate policy.

The short version

Dad Reflex does not ask for an account, an email address or a name. Your progress lives on your phone. What leaves the device is what you choose to send, a Game Center score or an anonymous profile for online duels, plus two advertising pieces: the ads are served by Google AdMob, both the optional rewarded videos in the store and the short ads between some levels, and the game includes Meta's (Facebook's) SDK so I can tell whether Meta ads bring in new players. Your phone's advertising identifier is only shared for that if you tap Allow on Apple's tracking prompt. I do not sell data and I do not share it with data brokers.

What stays on your device

Levels won and their results, your pillow collection, tokens and hearts, achievement progress and your settings are all stored in the app's own storage on your phone. I cannot see any of it. Deleting the app deletes it. If you back your phone up through iOS, that backup may include the app's data; that backup belongs to you and Apple, not to me.

Game Center, if you use it

Signing in to Game Center is optional and the game plays in full without it. If you are signed in, Dad Reflex sends your Survival time to the Survival leaderboard and reports achievement progress. Apple stores that data and shows the nickname you chose with Apple, not one the game assigns. It is covered by Apple's privacy policy, not this one. Decline Game Center and you lose nothing but the leaderboard.

Online duels

The first time you open Online, the game creates an anonymous account on the match server. No email address, no password, no name from you. That account holds a random identifier, a display name the server generates for you and that you can change, your rating, your experience level, your win, loss and draw counts, and a record of the matches you play: the round's seed, both scores, the number of misses, the timestamps and who won.

That is the whole record. The game does not read your contacts, your photos, your location or your address book. The match server is hosted on Supabase. If you never open Online, no account is ever created and nothing is ever sent.

Purchases

Every purchase in Dad Reflex is processed by Apple through the App Store. The game is told that a purchase completed and which item it was for, so it can deliver it. I never see your card number, your billing address or your Apple Account details, and they are never stored.

Advertising

Ads. Dad Reflex shows ads served by Google AdMob in two ways. The rewarded videos are optional: the ones you choose to watch in the store to earn tokens, and nothing plays unless you ask for it. The other kind is a short full-screen ad in the gaps of the game: between some levels, sometimes when you retry one, before some online quick matches and after some Survival runs. Those never interrupt play and earn you nothing; they just show and the game carries on. To serve and measure either kind Google receives device and app information (model, iOS version, language, the app's version, an identifier for the device) and how the ad was shown and interacted with. Where the law requires consent, Google's consent form appears before the first ad, and you can change your answer later from the Privacy Options row in the game's settings. Google's handling of that data is described in Google's privacy policy. The permanent ad-free purchase removes the ads between levels and matches for good; the rewarded videos stay available, because you are the one who asks for them.

Meta install measurement. The game includes the Meta (Facebook) SDK so I can measure whether Meta ads lead to installs. It tells Meta when the app is installed and launched, when an in-app purchase is made (the item and price, never your card or Apple Account), and sends device and app information such as the phone model, iOS version, language, time zone and the app's version. Your phone's advertising identifier is only shared with Meta if you tap Allow on Apple's tracking prompt; tap Ask App Not to Track and it is never read. You can change your mind at any time in iPhone Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking. Meta's handling of that data is described in Meta's privacy policy. The game uses no Facebook login and shares nothing to Facebook.

Analytics

The Meta SDK described above logs a few app events, installs, launches and purchases, so that Meta can measure its ads; it may also send Meta a report if the app crashes inside Meta's own code. Beyond that there is no analytics SDK and no crash-reporting service: I do not track how you play.

Children

Dad Reflex is a game about catching babies on a pillow and it is built to be family-friendly. I do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, children included. The data that leaves a device is the anonymous online profile and the advertising measurement described above; none of it contains a name, an email address or anything else that identifies a person, and the advertising identifier is only used if the person holding the phone allows it.

Your choices

Skip Game Center and nothing goes to Apple. Skip Online and no account is created. Tap Ask App Not to Track when the game asks, or turn it off later in iPhone Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking, and your advertising identifier is never shared with Meta or Google. Buy the ad-free pack and the ads between levels and matches are gone for good; only the rewarded videos you ask for remain. Delete the app and the local save goes with it. To have an online profile and its match records deleted, email me from the device and tell me the display name on the profile; I will remove it.

Changes

If this policy changes, the effective date at the top changes with it, and anything that affects what is collected will be in place before the build that does it reaches the App Store.
