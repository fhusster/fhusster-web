Timing-based tower builder

Leave No Gaps

Pieces fall, you turn them to fit, and the camera never waits. Fill the tower to the line without a single gap and the whole thing blows at once. Every piece on the way up is its own small reward. Built for iPhone.

The Game

A tower five columns wide, a piece falling toward it, and a camera climbing the screen on its own. The game chooses where each piece lands; you choose how it is turned. Swipe left or right to rotate, swipe up to flip it over, tap to mirror it, swipe down to hurry it along. A ghost under the piece tells you the truth before it lands: solid means it fits, a red cross means it would leave a gap. Land it right and the piece welds into the tower. Land it wrong and the top row under it bursts, the piece's cubes tumble into whatever is left, and the camera keeps climbing while you recover. Mistakes cost more each time, so a clean run is worth protecting.

The payoff is held back on purpose. You do not clear lines as you go. You build the whole tower, row by row, chasing a camera that never stops, until the core stands full to the finish line. Only then does it go: every cube in the tower bursts in one go, the lights along the sides switch off from the top down, and the level is yours. The longer the climb, the bigger the release, and a level won without a single miss sends the lights wild before the blast.

The climb itself is where most of the pleasure lives. Every correct placement lands with a pop, a squash and a pulse that runs up the core. Five in a row lights a row of bulbs up both sides of the tower, and each combo after that runs a light around every piece you have placed. An announcer calls out every fifth in a row and gets more impressed the longer you keep it going, all the way to a hundred. When things go wrong he is on your side too, with a word of consolation for a streak cut short and something encouraging when the misses start to pile up. Nothing waits for any of it. The camera keeps rising, the next piece is already on its way, and the tower keeps growing under your thumb.

Ways to Build

  • 100 Levels

    Every level has its own starting foundation, its own colour and its own finish line. The early levels teach the turns. The mirror tap arrives at level 11, pieces that only fit one way of two arrive at level 40, and by level 100 almost every piece asks for a decision.

  • Endless

    No finish line. The camera speeds up on a clock, from level 7's pace to level 100's in seven minutes and on past it, while the tower fades from one colour to the next. Your height is your score, and your best run is kept.

  • Daily Challenge

    One challenge a day, the same for everyone, drawn from 97 twists on the rules: a core that narrows as it climbs, a hidden NEXT, a ghost that keeps its verdict to itself, pieces that carry a bomb, a run that ends on the first miss. Each one is rated easy, medium or hard before you start.
